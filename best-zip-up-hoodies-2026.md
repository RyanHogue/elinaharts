Pullover hoodies dominated the last few years. For 2026, industry trend coverage points to the
**zip-up making a real comeback** — the reason cited most often is practical, not aesthetic: a zip
is easier to layer over a t-shirt and easier to take off without messing up your hair or ruining a
throw-on look. It's also just a more useful garment through a season where the morning is 60°F and
the afternoon is 80°F, which is exactly what September looks like across most of the US.

publish, ranked by the one number that actually predicts how it will feel: **fabric weight, in
GSM** (grams per square metre). The range runs from a light 230 GSM transitional jacket to a
750 GSM piece that's closer to outerwear than sweatshirt.

## Full-zip vs. quarter-zip: not the same garment

- **Full-zip** opens all the way — easiest to layer, easiest to vent when it's warmer than
  expected, closest to a jacket replacement.
- **Quarter-zip (half-zip)** only opens partway at the collar — better wind-blocking at the neck,
  usually a pullover fit through the body, closer to a sweater than a jacket.

Both showed up in every brand's catalog we checked. If you're buying for "layer over a t-shirt,
take off in the car," go full-zip. If you're buying for "warm neck, pullover otherwise," a
quarter-zip does that job better.

## Who should NOT buy a zip hoodie

- ❌ **You want maximum warmth with zero draft.** A zip, even closed, lets more air through at the
  seam than a solid pullover. For the coldest days, a heavyweight pullover hoodie wins.
- ❌ **You're shopping purely on GSM.** A 417 GSM polyester-sherpa zip and a 417 GSM cotton zip are
  different garments that wear completely differently. Read the fibre, not just the number.
- ❌ **You need a specific size above 2XL.** Every zip hoodie on this list tops out at XL or 2XL —
  none of the brands we track currently sell an extended-size zip piece.
- ❌ **It's actually hot where you live.** Even the lightest pick here (230 GSM) is a light jacket,
  not a t-shirt replacement. Wait for real transitional weather.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are zip-up hoodies trending in 2026?**
Industry apparel-trend coverage attributes it mainly to practicality: a full zip is easier to layer
over other clothes and easier to remove without disturbing hair or makeup, which matters more as
oversized, layered outfits stay popular.

**What's the difference between a full-zip and a quarter-zip?**
Full-zip opens completely, works like a light jacket. Quarter-zip (half-zip) only opens partway at
the collar — better at blocking wind at the neck, otherwise worn like a pullover.

**What GSM should I buy for September?**
For most of the US, September is transitional — 230–350 GSM covers cool mornings without
overheating by afternoon. Save the 500+ GSM pieces for when it's consistently cold.

**Is the 750 GSM quarter-zip really that much heavier than a "normal" heavyweight hoodie?**
Yes. Most hoodies marketed as heavyweight sit at 400–530 GSM. At 750 GSM, this piece is nearly 50%
denser than even Jamroc's own 530 GSM flagship — brand-published, oz-to-GSM math checks out, but we
have not independently verified it beyond the brand's own listing.

**Do any of these ship to the US?**
All three brands run direct-to-consumer US storefronts. Confirm shipping and current stock at
checkout before ordering.

**Which is the best value?**
On price-per-gram, Farm Brand's sherpa zip ($14/100 GSM). On genuine heavyweight *cotton*, Jamroc's
$110 Culture Club Zip is the cheapest way in.
